# Maintainers

Who can approve a change to the Cabuya Protocol, and which application each of
them represents.

This file exists so the composition limit in [`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md) is
**auditable by anyone, at any time, without asking**. At most two maintainers
per application or organisation, counting anyone employed by, contracting for,
or founding it. If this table ever shows three seats from one application, that
is a governance violation you can point at in a pull request.

## Current

| Maintainer | Application represented | Since |
|---|---|---|
| *(the initial maintainer team)* | Corag | 2026-08 |

**The council is not seated.** The protocol is maintained by the team that wrote
the 0.1 draft, and this table says so rather than listing names to look
established. It fills in as people meet the criteria.

Individuals are listed by their public handle when they join, with the
application they represent. The application matters more than the person here:
the composition limit counts organisations, and a reader checking neutrality
needs to see which ones are at the table.

## The release blocker

> `Cabuya 1.0` cannot be tagged until at least two maintainers in this table
> represent applications other than Corag.

Until that is true, the specification stays at `0.x` and every artefact says
*draft*. This is why the table above is short and honest rather than padded: the
gate is only worth something if the list it reads from is real.

## Becoming one

Four requirements, in [`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md#becoming-a-maintainer).
The first one is the one that matters: **your application publishes a conforming
feed that the public validator passes.** Shipping qualifies; enthusiasm does
not.

## Emeritus

Nobody yet. A maintainer inactive for 120 days moves here automatically — no
vote, no drama, restorable on request.
