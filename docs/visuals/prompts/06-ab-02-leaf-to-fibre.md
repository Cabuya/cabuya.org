# AB-02 — Leaf to fibre

- **Target surface:** `/about` → the section arguing the metaphor is exact rather than decorative
- **Type:** illustration
- **Priority:** high
- **Purpose:** The section's claim is that cabuya is *made*, not found — many weak fibres twisted until they hold a load none of them could hold alone, which is the literal proposition being put to a network of separate applications. A plate that shows the four states in sequence proves it faster than the paragraph does, and it is the image the whole brand thesis rests on.
- **Aspect & dimensions:** 3:2 · 720 × 480 displayed · generate @2x (1440 × 960)
- **Light/dark strategy:** transparent
- **Prompt:**
  > **HOUSE STYLE —** Fine hand-drawn line work in the manner of a botanical plate or a rope-maker's technical drawing: twisted fibres, braided cords, and threads that bind. Line weight varies the way real cordage does — a thick laid rope resolving into thin individual filaments, each filament drawn as its own stroke. Flat 2D, no perspective, no lighting, no photographic reference. Print-quality line work with a faint natural paper tooth, as if pulled from a plate in a manual on cordage.
  >
  > **ONE FILE, BOTH THEMES —** The drawing is placed on a warm-white page (`#FAF9F6`) *and* on a dark green one (`#082A24`), from the same file, so it must carry its own tonal range instead of borrowing the page's. Draw it the way an engraving reads on either paper: deep forest-ink (`#0B3D32`) contours and shadow, with ivory (`#F6F3ED`) highlights worked into the forms themselves. A drawing in one flat value disappears on one of the two grounds.
  >
  > **SUBJECT —** A landscape plate showing **one continuous transformation in four stages, read left to right**, each stage flowing into the next rather than sitting in its own box:
  >
  > 1. **A cut fique leaf**, seen edge-on, its parallel veins visible along its length.
  > 2. **The leaf opening into loose fibre** — the veins separating into dozens of fine, slightly wavy individual strands, splayed and unruly. This is the stage that has to look weak: single hairs, no order, nothing that could hold anything.
  > 3. **The strands gathering and twisting** — the fibres drawing together and beginning to spiral, some over, some under, the disorder resolving into direction.
  > 4. **A finished laid rope** — three unmistakable strands, thick and regular, continuing to the right and tapering to nothing inside the frame.
  >
  > The point of the sequence is the contrast between stages 2 and 4: the same material, and only the twist between them. Give stage 2 real width and looseness so the eye registers how little it is, and stage 4 real density so the eye registers how much it became.
  >
  > **Exactly one fibre is fique gold (`#C79A4A`)** — traceable from inside the leaf's veins in stage 1, through the loose splay, through the twist, and still identifiable inside the finished rope. One continuous thread, drawn as a thread. It is the only chroma in the image. If you cannot follow the single golden fibre across all four stages, the drawing is wrong.
  >
  > **EDGES / BACKGROUND —** Fully transparent ground: no page colour, no fill, no paper rectangle, no border, no frame, no vignette, no panel behind any stage. **Nothing touches or crosses an edge** — the leaf at the left and the rope at the right both terminate inside the canvas and feather to nothing, with a clear margin of at least 8% of the canvas on all four sides containing no ink at all.
  >
  > **AVOID —** any border, frame, rectangle, box, outline or panel; dividing lines or arrows between the stages; numbers, captions or callouts; a filled or tinted ground; elements near or touching the edges; **any text, letters, numerals or labels whatsoever**; human figures, hands, faces or tools being held; machinery; photographic realism; 3D render; glossy or metallic surfaces; drop shadows; a second accent colour; neon or saturated colour; clip-art; flat-vector illustration; watermark.
