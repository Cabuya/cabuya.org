> dimensions (§5), asset paths (§6), accessibility (§7), the negative

- **Target surface:** `src/components/home/Hero.astro` → right column, visible from `lg`
- **Type:** illustration
- **Priority:** flagship
- **Purpose:** The first and often only image a reader sees. It has to carry the whole argument before they read a word: many separate thin threads, arriving from different directions, twisting into one rope that could actually hold a load. One of the threads is gold — the reader's app, or any app, it does not matter which. If this image works, the headline is a caption.
- **Aspect & dimensions:** 4:5 portrait · 520 × 650 displayed · generate @2x (1040 × 1300)
- **Light/dark strategy:** ~~matched pair~~ → **transparent, one asset.** *(Revised at integration.)* The generated art carries both forest ink and ivory highlights, so a single alpha asset reads correctly on `#FAF9F6` and on `#082A24` — verified by compositing against both token grounds. It is also the only way the corner-hex rule can hold: every lossy WebP of a flattened ground landed one to three steps off (`#FAF9F6` → `#FBF9F5`, `#082A24` → `#092A23`), and near-lossless cost 295 KB to fix it. With alpha, the ground *is* the page.
- **Prompt:**
  > **HOUSE STYLE —** Fine hand-drawn line work in the manner of a botanical plate or a rope-maker's technical drawing: twisted fibres, braided cords, knots, and threads that bind. Line weight varies the way real cordage does — a thick laid rope resolving into thin individual filaments. Forest ink (`#0B3D32`) deepening to near-black in the shadows, with ivory (`#F6F3ED`) highlights inside the forms, all on a fully transparent ground, and **one single fique-gold strand** (`#C79A4A`) traced through the composition — one continuous thread, never a fill, never a second accent. Calm, warm, technical, editorial. Flat 2D. Generous margins. Print-quality line work; any paper tooth is drawn into the strokes themselves, never laid down as a background wash.
  >
  > **ONE FILE, BOTH THEMES —** The drawing is placed on a warm-white page (`#FAF9F6`) *and* on a dark green one (`#082A24`), from the same file, so it must carry its own tonal range instead of borrowing the page's. Draw it the way an engraving reads on either paper: deep forest-ink (`#0B3D32`) contours and shadow, with ivory (`#F6F3ED`) highlights worked into the forms themselves. A drawing in one flat value disappears on one of the two grounds.
  >
  > **SUBJECT —** Roughly a dozen fine separate threads enter from the lower third of the frame at different angles, each drawn with its own character — some slack, some taut, some frayed at the end. As they rise they converge and twist into a single laid rope that continues out of the top of the composition, thick and evidently strong. The transition from loose threads to cordage happens in the middle third and is the focus of the drawing: you can see individual filaments still visible inside the twist. Exactly one thread is fique gold (`#C79A4A`) and is traceable by eye from its loose end at the bottom into the rope, where it remains just visible among the forest-ink filaments. No hands, no plant, no ground plane, no horizon — the threads and the rope are the entire subject, floating in the paper.
  >
  > **EDGES / BACKGROUND —** **True transparent background (alpha channel). No fill of any colour** — not white, not warm white, not a near-transparent tint, not a paper texture laid edge to edge. The page supplies the ground; anything baked in shows up as a rectangle in one of the two themes.
  >
  > **NOTHING MAY TOUCH OR CROSS AN EDGE.** The single exception is the **top** edge, where the rope deliberately continues out of frame — see the subject. The **left, right and bottom** edges must be clear. Leave a clear margin of **at least 8% of the canvas** on those edges with no ink in it at all: no thread, no fibre tip, no stray mark, no faint tail. A composition that runs to the border and is cut is rejected and regenerated — the asset gets scaled, and sometimes cropped, on pages we do not control, and a cut edge reads as a broken image rather than a drawing.
  >
  > **AVOID —** **any element touching, crossing or clipped by an edge of the canvas**; a full-bleed composition; any background fill, ground colour or paper rectangle — including white; a vignette or darkened border; any border, frame, rectangle, box or outline around the image; hard straight edges; gradients other than the soft fade into the background; glossy or 3D render; photographic realism; neon or saturated colour; drop shadows; busy or cluttered composition; watermark or signature; any logo; **any text, letters, numerals or labels whatsoever**; garbled glyphs; flat-vector "corporate memphis" people; clip-art; emoji; a second accent colour; human faces or identifiable people; disaster imagery of any kind.

A plain `<img>` with `srcset`, **never** a `<picture>` with a `prefers-color-scheme` source: the asset is transparent, and a `<source>` pointing at a `-dark` file that does not exist breaks the image in dark mode. `sizes="(min-width: 1024px) 40vw, 1px"` — the column is height-driven, and the `1px` branch keeps the 2x file off the screens where it never renders. - **Accepted with three deviations,** all judged and none blocking:
  1. **Reading direction.** The prompt asked for threads rising and converging out of the top; the art reads top-down, a laid rope splaying into filaments like a root system. The same argument, and a stronger one for a fique project — but it can be read as *one thing fraying* rather than *many becoming one*. Kept, because the gold strand runs continuously from the loose end into the twist and settles the direction for anyone who follows it.
  2. **Weight.** The 2x file is 172 KB against the guide's 120 KB hero target. The alpha channel, not the colour data, is the cost — quality 82 → 60 moves it by 48 KB. 1x devices fetch 61 KB.
  3. **Right edge.** 26 pixels of one filament graze the right border instead of feathering out. Invisible at display size; fix in a regeneration, not with a crop, which would break the 4:5.
- **Verified:** four corners fully transparent (alpha 0) · zero text or glyphs · exactly one gold strand, traceable end to end · reads at 520 px · ratio 0.8003 vs 0.8 target.
- **Re-framed and re-cut, 2026-08-17.** Two files now, both from the same master and both produced by `scripts/build-illustrations.mjs`: `hero-cordage.webp` 520 × 589 (75 KB) + `-2x` 1040 × 1178 (204 KB) for `lg` and up, padded on three sides so the rope still bleeds off the top edge and reads as descending out of the site header; and `hero-cordage-full.webp` 480 × 543 (58 KB) + `-2x` for everything narrower, padded on all four. **The drawing renders on every viewport now** — it was `hidden lg:flex`, which meant the site's flagship image did not exist for a phone, and then briefly a bottom crop under a mask, which read as trimmed. The two are served through one `<picture>` with a `media` source, so exactly one file is fetched on the LCP surface, and they share an aspect ratio by construction so the single declared box is right for both.
