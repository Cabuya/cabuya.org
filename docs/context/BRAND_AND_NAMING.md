# Brand & Naming Strategy — the unified aid-data protocol

> **TL;DR**
> The brand must survive three identity phases without a rename: emergency aid network → civic-tech interop standard → regional tech movement. That single requirement kills most of the obvious names, because *"ayuda"*, *"Colombia"*, *"Pereira"* and *"sismo"* are all phase-1 words.
> **Recommended #1: `Cabuya`** — the fique fibre rope of the Andes, the thing you use to tie together what one person cannot carry alone. It is the only candidate of 47 checked that came back `likely available` on `.org`, `.dev`, `.app`, `.co`, `.net` **and** `.io`, with the GitHub handle and the npm package name also `likely available`.
> Shortlist for the vote: **Cabuya · Cardumen · Guadua · Convite · Trenzia**. Brand architecture: one master brand, protocol as a named sub-brand (`Protocolo Cabuya` / `Cabuya Protocol`).
> **Nothing has been registered.** Every availability verdict below is `likely available / taken / unclear` — never certain — with the endpoint and UTC timestamp that produced it.
> **This is a prepared decision, not a made one. The working group votes.**

**Inputs:** plan README (north-star vision, strategic priors) · `5.task_governance_naming_licensing.md` · `src/content/ecosystem-apps/*.yaml` (20 apps, collision set) · `docs/BRAND_GUIDE.md` + `docs/MESSAGING.md` (craft reference only — Corag's identity is explicitly **not** inherited) · live RDAP / GitHub / npm / web lookups, all 2026-08-16 UTC.

## 1. Brand foundation — what this brand must mean, before it has a name

### 1.1 The problem the brand has to solve

The protocol's neutrality is a **social** fact before it is a technical one. Twenty volunteer teams, most of whom have never met, have to be willing to put a badge on their own product. A badge is a small act of loyalty. People do not give loyalty to a hyphenated acronym, and they do not give it to a name that visibly belongs to someone else in the room.

So the name has one job that no schema can do: **make twenty teams feel like co-owners of something that outranks all twenty of them.**

### 1.2 The five naming territories

| # | Territory | The story it tells | Fits phase 1 / 2 / 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** | **The fibre / the weave** — *tejido, trenza, cabuya, urdimbre, telar* | Many separate threads; none of them is the rope. The rope is what they become. Infrastructure, not authorship. | 1 ✓ · 2 ✓ · 3 ✓ |
| **T2** | **The bridge / the channel** — *puente, cauce, vado, nexo, andamio* | Apps that feed each other. Something built so traffic can cross. | 1 ✓ · 2 ✓ · 3 ~ |
| **T3** | **The regional collaboration ritual** — *minga, convite, cayapa, tequio, ayni, ayllu* | A community that organized itself before anyone offered to help. Deep cultural legitimacy. | 1 ✓ · 2 ~ · 3 ~ |
| **T4** | **The collective natural form** — *cardumen, colmena, ceiba, guadua, semilla* | Many small bodies moving as one shape, with no leader and no centre. | 1 ✓ · 2 ✓ · 3 ✓ |
| **T5** | **The open standard / the network** — *relay, mesh, lattice, weft, commons, nodal* | Technical trust and durability. Reads as infrastructure from the first second. | 1 ~ · 2 ✓ · 3 ~ |
| **T6** | **Coined / hybrid** — *trenzia, convia, trenzal, cabuyar, hilvana* | No inherited baggage, total semantic freedom, but zero free meaning. | 1 ✓ · 2 ✓ · 3 ✓ |

### 1.3 Emotional brief

**Must carry**

- **Confianza técnica** — a developer reading the name should expect a spec, not a campaign.
- **Pertenencia** — twenty teams must be able to say *"estamos en X"* without it sounding like they joined someone else's company.
- **Seriedad sin solemnidad** — this is infrastructure people will still be using when the emergency is a memory.
- **Continuidad** — the sense of something that keeps working after the news cycle moves on.

**Must avoid**

- **Hype.** No *revolucionario*, no *disruptivo*, no *unificamos el ecosistema*.
- **Charity marketing.** No *manos que ayudan*, no *corazones*, no *juntos podemos*.
- **Heroism and savior framing.** The people in the data are neighbours, not beneficiaries of anyone's virtue.
- **Single-city or single-country ownership.** *Pereira*, *Risaralda*, *Colombia*, *Eje Cafetero* are all disqualifying as the name itself.
- **Emergency-tie.** *Sismo*, *terremoto*, *emergencia*, *SOS* date the brand to August 2026 and make it obsolete by design.
- **App-tie.** Nothing that reads as a Corag product. The whole point is that Corag is one node among twenty.

### 1.4 The long-horizon test (north star)

The founder's stated ambition is that this collaboration seeds a lasting Colombian tech ecosystem — a *"Silicon Valley colombiano"* grown out of Pereira's post-earthquake dev community. Stated as **vision**; there is no traction claim here and none should ever be manufactured.

That ambition converts into a hard naming rule. Every candidate is tested against three phases:

| Phase | What the brand has to be | The failure it must avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Emergency aid network** (2026) | A flag twenty aid apps fly during a live crisis | Sounding corporate or abstract while people are sleeping outside |
| **2 — Civic-tech interop standard** (2027–2029) | A spec that a city government, a foundation and a bank's CSR team can each adopt without embarrassment | Sounding like a disaster-relief campaign that expired |
| **3 — Regional tech movement** (2030+) | A name a conference, a fellowship, a jobs board and a set of companies can all sit under | Being permanently the "earthquake thing" |

**A name that only works in phase 1 is disqualified regardless of its score.** This is the criterion that removes `ayudared`, `colombiaunida`, `sismonet`, `redayuda` and every variant of them before scoring starts.

## 2. Criteria, defined before any scoring

### 2.1 Brand strength (B1–B7, 0–5 each, 35 max)

| Code | Criterion | A 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **B1** | **Distinctive** — not a generic utility word | You could not have guessed it; it is not `conecta`, `red`, `link` |
| **B2** | **Memorable after one hearing** | Someone hears it at a meetup and types it correctly a week later |
| **B3** | **Pronounceable in ES *and* EN** | A Spanish speaker and an English speaker both say it without coaching |
| **B4** | **Spellable from hearing** — *the domain test* | No `z`/`s`, `b`/`v`, `c`/`k`, `y`/`ll` ambiguity for a Latin-American speaker |
| **B5** | **Story in one sentence** | The meaning lands in under ten words, and the meaning is the argument |
| **B6** | **Visually brandable** | You can picture the wordmark and the sticker |
| **B7** | **Durable across all three phases** | Works in 2026 and in 2036, in Pereira and outside Colombia |

### 2.2 Practical fitness (P1–P4, 0–5 each, 20 max)

| Code | Criterion | A 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | **Domain signal** — `.org` / `.dev` / `.app` | All three come back `likely available` |
| **P2** | **GitHub org + npm package** | Bare handle and bare package both `likely available` |
| **P3** | **Collision-free** | No clash with the 20 ecosystem apps, humanitarian standards/orgs, or a known tech or NGO brand |
| **P4** | **Neutral + technically well-formed** | ≤ 10 chars, ASCII, no accents, valid as `/.well-known/{name}.json`, npm name, handle; tied to no country, city, emergency or app |

### 2.3 Two hard gates

Scores rank; gates disqualify.

- **Gate A — availability.** A candidate scoring ≤ 1 on **P1** cannot be #1. There is no version of this project that starts by negotiating a domain purchase.
- **Gate B — collision.** A candidate scoring ≤ 1 on **P3** cannot be #1, whatever else it does. A humanitarian data protocol that shares a name with a humanitarian NGO is a permanent explanation tax.

### 2.4 TLD trade-offs

| TLD | For | Against | Verdict for this project |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`.org`** | Reads non-commercial and neutral; the default for standards bodies (`openreferral.org`, `matrix.org`, `schema.org`) | Nothing material | **Canonical.** This is the one that must be free. |
| **`.dev`** | Perfect for the developer portal; HSTS-preloaded, so HTTPS is mandatory by construction | Reads developer-only; wrong front door for a mayor's office | **Secondary** — the dev portal lives here. |
| **`.app`** | Also HSTS-preloaded; good for a demo or validator | Implies "an app", which is exactly what the protocol is not | **Defensive only.** |
| **`.co`** | Regionally meaningful; the ecosystem already lives there | Reads as Colombia, which undercuts phase-3 neutrality if made canonical | **Defensive**, redirecting to `.org`. |
| **`.io` / `.net`** | Familiar to developers | `.io` is expensive and has a contested administration history | **Skip unless cheap.** |

### 2.5 The collision set, checked explicitly

**The 20 ecosystem apps** (from `src/content/ecosystem-apps/*.yaml`): Encontrados.co · Gravitas · Corag Ayuda Directa · Encuentra tu mascota · Help Them Directly · AquíAyuda · **ayuda.red** · Acopio (alluda.online) · PereiraVive · Pereira Unida · Reporte CO · Pereira Ayuda · SOS Pereira 2026 · Mapa del terremoto · SOS Terremoto · Mapa de Ayuda — Gogó · Pereira Responde · Unidos por Pereira · Terremoto Colombia · SismoVision.

> **`ayuda.red` — explicit finding.** `https://ayuda.red/` returned **HTTP 200** at `2026-08-16T04:09:50Z`. It is a live directory app inside the ecosystem we are trying to serve. **Every `ayuda*` construction is therefore disqualified before scoring** — `ayudared`, `ayuda.dev`, `redayuda`, `ayudaprotocol`, `red-de-ayuda`, `ayudaverse` and any near-homophone. A protocol named after one of its own member apps is not neutral, and the confusion would be permanent and unfixable. This is not a close call; it is removed at the territory stage, which is why no `ayuda*` candidate appears in the matrix below.

**Humanitarian standards and orgs checked for clash:** HXL (Humanitarian eXchange Language) · HSDS / Open Referral (Human Services Data Specification) · Sahana Eden · IATI · Sphere · CHS · OCHA · ReliefWeb. None of the shortlisted names collides phonetically or semantically with any of them. **Note the opposite risk too:** a name that sounds like an existing standard would imply an endorsement we do not have, which Rule-0 forbids.

**Well-known tech brands:** deliberately avoided the `-ly`, `-ify`, `-hub`, `-stack`, `-flow` suffix families and anything within one edit of a large existing developer brand.

## 3. Candidates and verification

### 3.1 Method and its honest limits

| Check | Method | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Domain** | RDAP against the **authoritative** registry endpoint — `rdap.publicinterestregistry.org` for `.org`, `pubapi.registry.google` for `.dev`/`.app`, `rdap.org` bootstrap for `.co`/`.net`/`.io`. Verdict = `likely available` when the registry returns an RDAP 404 / `errorCode`, `taken` when it returns a `domain` object. | RDAP tells you the registry has no record **at the moment of the query**. It says nothing about premium pricing, registry reservations, ICANN reserved names, or a registration made five minutes later. **Never treat as certain.** |
| **GitHub org** | `GET https://github.com/{name}` — 404 → `likely available`, 200 → `taken`; owner type via `api.github.com/users/{name}` | A 404 does not guarantee the name is claimable — GitHub reserves some names, and org creation can still be refused. |
| **npm** | `GET https://registry.npmjs.org/{name}` — 404 → `likely available` | npm blocks names too similar to existing ones; a 404 is a signal, not a promise. |
| **Social handles** | Attempted `instagram.com/{name}` | **Failed as a method.** Every handle returned HTTP 302 (login redirect) at `2026-08-16T04:08:05–04:08:19Z`. Anonymous probing cannot distinguish taken from free, so **every social-handle verdict below is `unclear`** and must be checked by hand from a logged-in browser before the vote. |
| **Trademark** | Web search for existing marks and companies | Best-effort only. No USPTO/EUIPO/SIC database query was made. All trademark verdicts are `unclear`. |

**Sweep timestamps (UTC, 2026-08-16):** `.org` sweep `04:05:46 → 04:06:02` · GitHub + npm sweep `04:05:24 → 04:05:33` · `.dev`/`.app` serial sweep `04:06:35 → 04:09:05` · supplementary `.co`/`.net`/`.io` + extra candidates `04:06:37 → 04:09:30`.

### 3.2 Cultural verification of the regional collaboration words

The task file requires verifying the cultural meaning of regional words *before* proposing them. Four of the six were verified out of the running.

| Word | What it actually means | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Minga** (Quechua *mink'a*) | Pre-Columbian communal work tradition across the Andes from Chile to Colombia; RAE: *"reunión de amigos y vecinos para hacer algún trabajo gratuito en común"*. **But** in Colombia the word has a strong, current political charge: the *Minga Nacional de Resistencia Indígena y Popular* (2008) and the *minga indígena* mobilizations organized by ONIC and CRIC are a recognized form of protest and political action. | **Rejected on collision.** The meaning is perfect and the politics are unavoidable. A protocol whose entire proposition is neutrality cannot carry a name that half of Colombia reads as a political position. Sources: [Semana](https://www.semana.com/nacion/articulo/que-es-la-minga-indigena-y-por-que-marcha/202012/), [CRIC](https://www.cric-colombia.org/portal/por-que-nos-movilizamos-en-minga/), [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/america/colombia/2020/10/15/a-proposito-de-la-movilizacion-indigena-que-llegara-a-bogota-sabe-que-es-una-minga/) · checked 2026-08-16T04:03Z |
| **Convite** | Colombian communal work party: neighbours in a *vereda* or a *barrio* agree to build the thing themselves — housing, water, drainage, roads. Documented as the mechanism that built the peripheries of Medellín and much of rural Antioquia; described in the sources as literally constructing the *"tejido comunitario"*. | **Culturally excellent, kept in the shortlist with a warning.** Sources: [El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/antioquia/que-es-un-convite-historia-y-ejemplos-en-antioquia-AD13235050), [Unidad Solidaria](https://www.unidadsolidaria.gov.co/Prensa/Noticias-Tierra-de-convite) · checked 2026-08-16T04:07Z |
| **Cayapa** | Collective unpaid work, from the Cumanagoto language; mainly a Venezuelan usage. **Two problems:** it is also the exonym for the **Chachi people** of Esmeraldas, Ecuador — a name they explicitly rejected in 1978 in favour of *Chachi* — and *"Cayapa Heroica"* is a Venezuelan state programme run through a government body. | **Rejected.** Using a name an indigenous people asked to stop being called is not a risk worth taking, and the state-programme association imports foreign politics. Sources: [Wikipedia — Chachi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chachi_people), [Cendit](https://www.cendit.gob.ve/cayapa-heroica-venezolana/), [RAE](https://dle.rae.es/cayapa) · checked 2026-08-16T04:04Z |
| **Tequio** | Zapotec/Nahuatl-rooted communal labour obligation, specifically Mexican and in several states a legally codified duty. | **Rejected.** Culturally Mexican, not Andean; borrowing it reads as costume. |
| **Ayni / Ayllu** | Quechua/Aymara reciprocity (*ayni*) and the kin-based collective landholding community (*ayllu*). Both are living, sacred-adjacent concepts in Peru, Bolivia and Ecuador. | **Rejected.** Not Colombian, and appropriating a living Andean cosmological term for a JSON schema is the kind of decision that ages badly. |
| **Aluna** | The Kogi/Arhuaco concept of the originating thought-force, from the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta. | **Rejected before checking availability.** Sacred concept of a living people; not ours to brand. |

**Cabuya, verified.** *Cabuya* is the fibre — and the rope made from it — extracted from *fique* (also *pita*, *penco*, *henequén*). **Colombia is the world's largest grower of fique.** RAE and Latin-American dictionaries record two idioms that matter here:

- **"Coger la cabuya" / "ponerse en la cabuya"** — *to catch on, to get in the loop, to pick up the thread of a matter.* This is close to a gift: a protocol whose adoption verb already exists in the language.
- **"Estar en la cabuya"** (Colombia, Ecuador) — *to be in a jam you cannot easily get out of.* This is the honest downside, stated in §5.1 and not buried.

Sources: [RAE — cabuya](https://dle.rae.es/cabuya), [Semana — La cabuya](https://www.semana.com/especiales/articulo/la-cabuya/79581-3/), [Wikipedia — Fique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fique) · checked 2026-08-16T04:07Z

**Guadua, verified.** *Guadua angustifolia* is the native Andean bamboo of the coffee region and the structural material of *bahareque*. The sources are specific about the earthquake connection: bahareque built with guadua **demonstrated adequate seismic behaviour in the 1999 Armenia earthquake**, though the vernacular knowledge was largely ignored during the reconstruction; the Puente de Guadua at the exit of Calle 80 became the symbol of the region's rebuilding. Sources: [Politika](https://politika.com.co/dos-guaduas-dos-culturas-dos-bahareques-arq-jaime-mogollon-seba/), [180 Grados Quindío](https://180gradosquindio.com/terremoto-armenia-27-anos/) · checked 2026-08-16T04:05Z

## 5. Shortlist — five brand cases for the vote

### 5.1 `Cabuya` — recommended #1

**Territory:** T1, the fibre / the weave.

**The story, in one sentence**
> *La cabuya es la fibra con la que se amarra lo que nadie puede cargar solo.*
> *Cabuya is the fibre you use to tie together what no one can carry alone.*

**Why it works, and keeps working**

The metaphor is exact rather than decorative. A protocol is not the apps; it is the cordage between them. Cabuya is made by twisting many weak fibres until they hold a load none of them could hold — which is the literal proposition being put to twenty volunteer teams. It is a working material, not a symbol: unglamorous, cheap, ubiquitous, and load-bearing. That register is right for infrastructure and wrong for a campaign, which is what we want.

It is Colombian in a way that is a source of pride rather than a boundary: Colombia is the world's largest grower of fique, and the fibre exists across the Andes and Central America, so the name travels. And it is tied to **no** city, **no** emergency and **no** app.

The idiom is nearly a gift. **"Coger la cabuya"** already means *to get in the loop, to pick up the thread*. The adoption verb was in the language before the protocol existed: *"¿Ya le cogiste la cabuya?"*

**Phase test**

| Phase | How it reads |
|---|---|
| 1 — Emergency network | *"Cabuya conecta las apps de ayuda."* Concrete, humble, immediately understood in Spanish. |
| 2 — Interop standard | *"Protocolo Cabuya 1.0"* on a government procurement document reads like a real standard, not a relief campaign. |
| 3 — Regional tech movement | *Comunidad Cabuya*, *Cabuya Conf*, *Cabuya Fellowship* — a movement named after the fibre a region has always made. It absorbs new meaning instead of expiring. |

**Taglines**

| | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | **«La fibra común de las apps que ayudan.»** | **"The common fibre of the apps that help."** |
| Secondary | **«Cada app, un hilo. Juntas, una cabuya.»** | **"Every app a thread. Together, a rope."** |
| Developer-facing | **«Un formato. Una tarde de trabajo. Ya estás conectado.»** | **"One format. One afternoon. You're connected."** |

**How the badge and the attribution read**

```
Compatible con Cabuya 1.0        Cabuya 1.0 compatible
Publica datos en Cabuya          Publishes Cabuya feeds
Lee feeds Cabuya                 Reads Cabuya feeds
```

Deliberately **not** *"Powered by Cabuya"* in Spanish — *"Impulsado por"* is marketing register and overstates the protocol's role. The protocol does not power anything; the app does the work and speaks the format. **«Compatible con Cabuya 1.0»** is the honest claim, it is version-scoped, and it is machine-verifiable against the validator, which is what makes a badge worth anything (see `GOVERNANCE_AND_LICENSING.md` §5.5).

**Domains — primary and defensive**

| Priority | Domain | Signal (2026-08-16) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Register first** | `cabuya.org` | **likely available** | Canonical: spec, registry, landing |
| **Register first** | `cabuya.dev` | **likely available** | Developer portal |
| Defensive | `cabuya.co` | **likely available** | Regional; 301 to `.org` |
| Defensive | `cabuya.app` | **likely available** | Validator / demo; 301 to `.org` |
| Optional | `cabuya.net` | **likely available** | Typo defence only |
| Skip | `cabuya.io` | **likely available** | Expensive; no added value |

`github.com/cabuya` — **likely available** · npm `cabuya` — **likely available** · fallback org `cabuya-protocol` — **likely available** · social handles — **unclear**, must be checked manually before the vote.

Path segment: `/.well-known/cabuya.json` — 6 ASCII chars, lowercase, no accents, no hyphen. Clean.

**Weaknesses, stated honestly**

1. **"Estar en la cabuya" means to be in a jam** in Colombia and Ecuador. Some people will make the joke. The mitigation is to lead with *"coger la cabuya"* in all launch copy so the positive idiom is the one that anchors — but the negative one exists and the group should decide with it in view.
2. **English speakers need one pronunciation cue.** `ka-BOO-ya`. Three syllables is at the limit of the brief's ideal. Put the pronunciation in the README's first paragraph and it stops being a problem; do not put it in and it becomes a small, permanent friction.
3. **The metaphor needs one sentence of explanation** for anyone outside the Andes. That sentence is good copy, so the cost is low — but it is a cost.
4. **Cabuya is a village in Puntarenas, Costa Rica.** No brand or sector collision, but it will appear in search results.
5. **Trademark status is `unclear`.** No register was queried. A proper clearance search in Colombia (SIC) and the US must happen before any money is spent on identity design.

### 5.2 `Cardumen` — #2

**Territory:** T4. **Story:** *Un cardumen es mucha gente pequeña moviéndose como un solo cuerpo, sin que nadie mande.* — *A school of fish: many small bodies, one shape, no one in charge.*

The most accurate description of what this network actually is: no centre, no leader, and the shape holds anyway. Completely neutral — tied to no country, city, emergency or app — and phonetically clean in both languages (`car-DU-men`).

**Taglines:** ES «Muchas apps, un solo movimiento.» / EN "Many apps, one movement." **Badge:** «Compatible con Cardumen 1.0».
**Domains:** `cardumen.app` **likely available**, `cardumen.co` **likely available**; `cardumen.org` and `.dev` both **taken**. GitHub `cardumen` **taken** (personal user) → `cardumen-protocol` **likely available**. npm `cardumen` **likely available**.

**Weaknesses:** `.org` is gone, and for a standards project `.org` is the address people guess — that is the whole gate-A argument. `.app` as the canonical domain says "this is an app", which is precisely the wrong claim. English speakers do not know the word. And the metaphor is about safety in numbers, not about *data moving between systems*, so it is warm but slightly off-target.

### 5.3 `Guadua` — #3

**Territory:** T4. **Story:** *La guadua es con lo que el Eje Cafetero se volvió a levantar.* — *Guadua is what the coffee region rebuilt itself with.*

The most emotionally loaded name on the list, and the evidence is real: guadua-framed *bahareque* performed adequately in the 1999 Armenia earthquake, and the Puente de Guadua became the symbol of that reconstruction. Guadua also grows as a connected stand — individually hollow, collectively rigid — which is a genuinely good network metaphor. For a phase-3 "regional tech movement" it is arguably the strongest name here.

**Taglines:** ES «Con lo que se reconstruye.» / EN "What we rebuild with." **Badge:** «Compatible con Guadua 1.0».
**Domains:** `guadua.dev` **likely available**, `guadua.co` **likely available**; `guadua.org` and `.app` **taken**. GitHub `guadua` **taken** (personal user) → `guadua-protocol` **likely available**. npm `guadua` **likely available**.

**Weaknesses:** the biggest one is the thing that makes it good — it is **strongly Eje-Cafetero-coded**, which is a version of the single-region ownership the brief asks us to avoid, and it points at the 1999 and 2026 earthquakes rather than away from them. `.org` is taken. And `gua-` is a real pronunciation obstacle for English speakers (`GWA-dua`), which matters in phase 2 and 3 more than in phase 1.

### 5.4 `Convite` — #4

**Territory:** T3. **Story:** *Un convite es cuando los vecinos se ponen de acuerdo y construyen la obra ellos mismos.* — *A convite is when the neighbours agree and build the thing themselves.*

Culturally the best fit on the entire list. This is literally what happened: twenty teams built aid software in a week without waiting for permission, which is the definition of a convite. The sources describe convites as having built the housing, water and drainage of Medellín's peripheries — and as constructing the *tejido comunitario*, the same phrase the protocol is trying to make machine-readable.

**Taglines:** ES «Lo construimos entre todos.» / EN "We build it between us." **Badge:** «Compatible con Convite 1.0».
**Domains:** `convite.dev` **likely available**, `convite.co` **likely available**; `convite.org` and `.app` **taken**. GitHub `convite` **taken** (personal user). npm `convite` **likely available**.

**Weaknesses — and this is why it is #4 rather than #1.** **Convite A.C. is an established Venezuelan humanitarian NGO**, founded 2006, working on human rights and health monitoring in exactly our sector, at `conviteac.org` / `conviteac.org.ve`, with an international profile (HelpAge network, AARP International, DevelopmentAid). Their own site defines the word as *"reciprocal aid, mutual help and work together"* — the same reading we would be claiming. That is **Gate B**: a permanent explanation tax, a real risk of implied association with an organization we have not verified and cannot speak for, and a live trademark question. Secondary weakness: in Portuguese and formal Spanish *convite* simply means *invitation* or *banquet*, which dilutes it. Kept on the shortlist because the group may weigh the cultural fit above the collision, but it should be voted on with the collision in plain sight.

### 5.5 `Trenzia` — #5

**Territory:** T6, coined. **Story:** *Hebras separadas que sólo sostienen porque se cruzan.* — *Separate strands that only hold because they cross.*

The clean-sweep option. `trenzia.org`, `.dev`, `.app`, `.co` all **likely available**; `github.com/trenzia` **likely available**; npm `trenzia` **likely available**. Being coined means zero inherited baggage, zero cultural verification risk, and total freedom to define the meaning — which is the standard argument for invented names.

**Taglines:** ES «Se sostiene porque se cruza.» / EN "It holds because it crosses." **Badge:** «Compatible con Trenzia 1.0».

**Weaknesses:** **the spelling test fails.** With Latin-American *seseo*, a listener hearing `trenzia` may reasonably write `trensia` — and a name that cannot be typed correctly from hearing is a bad domain, a bad `/.well-known/` path and a bad conference badge. Secondarily, coined names start with **no** free meaning: everything the brand means has to be paid for in explanation, which an all-volunteer project with no marketing budget cannot easily afford. And `-ia` reads faintly pharmaceutical. It is on the list because availability is real and the group deserves a low-risk option, not because it is the strongest brand.

### 5.6 The recommendation

**#1 is `Cabuya`**, for two reasons that do not depend on taste.

1. **It is the only candidate that clears both gates while scoring in the top tier on brand strength.** 32/35 brand, 19/20 practical, 51/55 total — seven points clear of the next candidate. Of 47 names checked, it is the only one with `.org` + `.dev` + `.app` + `.co` + GitHub + npm all `likely available`. That combination will not stay available indefinitely, and it removes the entire class of launch problems that begins with "we need to talk to whoever owns the domain".
2. **The metaphor is the argument.** The protocol's proposition is that many individually weak feeds, twisted together, hold a load none of them could hold alone. That is not a description of cabuya; that is what cabuya *is*. A name that argues for you is worth more than a name that merely sounds good.

**Second choice if the group rejects it:** `Cardumen`, on the strength of its neutrality — but the group must accept `.org` being unavailable, which is a real cost.

> **This is a prepared decision, not a made one.** The shortlist exists so that the working group has five defensible options with the evidence attached, not so that anyone can present a *fait accompli*. Every name here is proposed **for** the group to approve, and none of the twenty teams has agreed to anything. Nothing has been registered.

## 6. Brand architecture

### 6.1 The question

Two structures are available:

- **A — One master brand.** `Cabuya` is the movement, the community, the protocol, the tooling and the org. Products are descriptive sub-names: `Cabuya Spec`, `Cabuya Validator`, `Cabuya Registry`.
- **B — Movement master-brand + protocol sub-brand.** A movement name (say `Cabuya`) with a separately-named protocol (say `Hilo` or `CAP`) underneath it.

### 6.2 How real ecosystems did it

| Ecosystem | Structure | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **Open Referral / HSDS** | B — movement brand (*Open Referral*) + separately-named spec (*Human Services Data Specification*), founder-led with a lead organizer, a funded technical steward and a Technical Committee, fiscally hosted by Aspiration | The closest analogue to this project in domain, scale and governance shape. **Assessment (ours, not a cited finding):** the split spends brand equity twice — implementers cite `HSDS`, and the movement name accrues recognition more slowly than the artefact name does. |
| **Matrix / Element** | B — protocol (`Matrix`, under the Matrix.org Foundation with a Spec Core Team and Guardians) + commercial app (`Element`) | Worked, but only because the Foundation was **deliberately spun out** of the company precisely to fix the "unequal power dynamic" of a spec owned by one vendor. The separation is governance, not branding — and it took years. |
| **Bluesky / AT Protocol** | B — app brand (`Bluesky`) + protocol brand (`AT Protocol`) | The app brand overwhelmingly dominates public recognition; the protocol brand is known mainly to developers. |
| **Let's Encrypt / ACME / ISRG** | Three names: a public brand, an IETF protocol (RFC 8555) and a legal entity | Works because the public brand carries the whole story and nobody outside the field needs to know the other two. Requires a big enough project to sustain three identities. |
| **CNCF / Certified Kubernetes** | A — one strong product brand plus a **conformance mark** derived from it (`Certified Kubernetes`, a Linux Foundation trademark) | The mark is a sub-brand of the *same* name rather than a second name. This is the pattern our badge should copy. |

### 6.3 Recommendation: **A — one master brand, with the protocol as a named sub-brand of the same word**

This project has **zero brand equity** to spend and an all-volunteer comms capacity of approximately zero. Two brands would halve an already tiny budget of attention and force every adopter to learn two words instead of one. Matrix and Let's Encrypt could afford the split; a group of twenty volunteer teams in its first year cannot.

Concretely:

```
Cabuya                                  the movement, the community, the org
 ├── Protocolo Cabuya 1.0 / Cabuya Protocol 1.0    the normative spec
 ├── Cabuya Registry                    who publishes what, and validation status
 ├── Cabuya Validator                   the conformance checker
 ├── Cabuya Skill                       the agent skill that implements a feed
 ├── Cabuya MCP                         the reference MCP server
 └── Compatible con Cabuya 1.0          the conformance mark  ← the ONLY gated asset
```

The **conformance mark is the sub-brand**, exactly as `Certified Kubernetes` is a sub-brand of `Kubernetes` — one word to learn, one badge to earn, one registry to check it against.

**The upgrade path is pre-committed rather than improvised.** If the movement outgrows the protocol in phase 3 — a conference, a fellowship, a jobs board, several companies — the protocol is *already* separately named (`Protocolo Cabuya`) inside the master brand, so it can be spun into its own identity later without a rename. Deciding structure A now costs nothing that structure B would have preserved.

## 7. Verbal identity starter — `Cabuya`

### 7.1 Product naming conventions

| Product | Spanish | English | Repo / package |
|---|---|---|---|
| The specification | **Protocolo Cabuya 1.0** | **Cabuya Protocol 1.0** | `cabuya/spec` |
| The schemas | **esquemas Cabuya** | **Cabuya schemas** | `cabuya/spec` → `schemas/` |
| The conformance checker | **validador Cabuya** | **Cabuya Validator** | `cabuya/validator` · npm `cabuya-validator` |
| The directory of feeds | **registro Cabuya** | **Cabuya Registry** | `cabuya/registry` |
| The agent skill | **skill Cabuya** | **Cabuya Skill** | `cabuya/skill` |
| The MCP server | **servidor MCP de Cabuya** | **Cabuya MCP Server** | `cabuya/mcp-server` |
| The site + dev portal | **cabuya.org** / **cabuya.dev** | same | `cabuya/website` |
| A conforming feed | **un feed Cabuya** | **a Cabuya feed** | `/.well-known/cabuya.json` |
| The conformance mark | **«Compatible con Cabuya 1.0»** | **"Cabuya 1.0 compatible"** | — |

**Rules.** Lowercase everywhere it is a path, a package or a handle (`cabuya`, `/.well-known/cabuya.json`). Capitalised when it is the brand in prose (`Cabuya`). Never all-caps — `CABUYA` reads as an acronym and it is not one. Never possessive across the language boundary: *"el feed de Cabuya"* is wrong (the feed is the app's, not ours); *"un feed Cabuya"* is right. Version numbers are always attached in normative and badge contexts — `Cabuya 1.0`, never a bare `Cabuya` on a badge.

### 7.2 Elevator pitch

**Español**
> Cabuya es un formato abierto para que las apps de ayuda publiquen y lean los mismos datos: puntos de acopio, necesidades, capacidades y entregas.
> Cualquier equipo puede implementarlo en una tarde, y nadie tiene que pedirle permiso a nadie.

**English**
> Cabuya is an open format that lets aid apps publish and read the same data: collection points, needs, capacities and deliveries.
> Any team can implement it in an afternoon, and nobody has to ask anyone for permission.

### 7.3 Vocabulary to avoid

**Hype and marketing register:** *revolucionario · disruptivo · la plataforma líder · el estándar definitivo · unificamos el ecosistema · cambiamos la forma en que · empoderar · sinergia · solución integral · game changer · next-generation · seamless.*

**Charity and savior register:** *salvamos vidas · héroes · beneficiarios · los más vulnerables (as a label for people) · juntos podemos · manos que ayudan.*

**Claims we cannot back — Rule-0 applies to brand copy as much as to product copy:**
- Never *"las 20 apps del ecosistema usan Cabuya"* until a registry entry proves it, per app, with a validation timestamp.
- Never *"el estándar de ayuda de Colombia"* — nobody appointed us, and the country-tie is the thing we are avoiding.
- Never *"certificado"* for anything the validator has not actually checked.
- Never name or imply endorsement by an organization that has not agreed in writing.

**Phase-tie words that must never enter the brand vocabulary:** *terremoto · sismo · emergencia · SOS · Pereira · Risaralda · Eje Cafetero · Colombia* — as parts of the name, the tagline or the domain. They belong in the *history* section of the site, where they are true and where they age well.

**AI-slop scaffolding:** *"En un mundo donde…" · "No es solo X, es Y" · "Ya sea que… o…" · "la clave está en" · "imagina un futuro donde".*
