You have decided to start and you do not want to reach a foundation only to find they need no help, or that the waiting list runs to months.

The reality is that almost every organization needs volunteers permanently. Natural turnover, growing projects and busy seasons keep demand open nearly all the time.

The question is not who needs help. It is who needs exactly what you can give.

## The five sectors with the most demand

### Early childhood

Support for stimulation programmes, supplementary feeding and care for children under five. Demand is enormous, constant, and almost never met.

### Complementary education

School tutoring, extracurricular programmes, community libraries, homework spaces outside school hours.

They ask for two to four hours a week. It is the format that fits best around a job or a degree.

### Older people

The older population is growing fast and institutional resources are not. Care homes, day centres and home-visit programmes take volunteers gladly.

Loneliness is the central problem for this group, and a short but consistent presence changes their week.

### Environment

Ecosystem restoration, wildlife conservation, environmental education, monitoring of protected areas.

If you prefer outdoor work and discrete days rather than a weekly commitment, it is a very different experience from urban volunteering.

### Migrant populations

Support with regularisation, school integration, access to healthcare and finding work.

If you have legal, medical, psychological or teaching training, your contribution here has a direct and measurable effect.

## Large or small: the practical difference

Large foundations have formal processes and long timelines. They receive many applications and their list is full.

Small and mid-sized ones usually bring you in almost immediately, and are where your contribution counts for relatively more because fewer people are covering the same ground.

If you want to start soon, start there. With experience and references, the large ones open up afterwards.

## The gesture that sets you apart

Volunteer coordinators receive a great many forms and very few direct approaches.

If you call or write introducing yourself clearly — what you can offer and when you are available — you step out of the mass of applications that almost never convert. It is a small thing that usually produces much faster replies.

## Do not give up on the first "not right now"

Needs change with the season and with what projects are running. An organization with nothing today may be urgently looking in two months.

Keep a short list of the ones that interest you and check back periodically.
