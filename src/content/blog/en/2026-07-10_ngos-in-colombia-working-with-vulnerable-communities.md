When you think about organizations helping the most vulnerable, the familiar names come up: the ones in the media, the ones with a constant presence online.

Behind them are thousands of NGOs sustaining entire communities without ever appearing in a headline. And they are generally the ones that need support most.

## Where they operate

In marginalised neighbourhoods of the big cities. In isolated rural areas. In territories affected by the armed conflict. In Indigenous, Afro-Colombian and campesino communities.

Each with different methods, and all depending on somebody deciding to give them time, resources or reach.

## The eight sectors

**Children and adolescents.** Full protection for minors facing economic, emotional or rights-based vulnerability.

**Reconciliation and peace.** Rebuilding social fabric after decades of violence. It is invisible work and it determines whether a community trusts again.

**Women and gender.** Gender violence, sexual and reproductive rights, economic autonomy, political education.

**People with disabilities.** Educational and workplace inclusion, family support, adaptive technology. Enormous need and very little visibility relative to the size of the population.

**Environment.** Watersheds, high-altitude wetlands, native forests, ecosystem restoration. For anyone with ecological leanings, it offers some of the most complete volunteering experiences available.

**Migrants and refugees.** Grown considerably in recent years. They need direct support, translation, administrative work and psychosocial care continuously.

**Older people.** The population grows steadily and the resources allocated grow less. NGOs with consistent volunteers cover the gap.

**People living on the street.** Shelters, kitchens, reintegration processes, basic medical care.

## The kind of impact nobody sees

A small NGO in a rural area might accompany fifteen campesino families for eight years with agroecological training. It never appears in the media. Its annual budget is modest.

And those fifteen families transformed their household economy, their food security and their children's education.

That is the deep, quiet work thousands of organizations across the country sustain, and it rarely receives support proportional to what it produces.

## How they work inside

Small teams with limited resources sustaining projects that cover thousands of people.

The operational efficiency of many Colombian NGOs is remarkable: they achieve a great deal on modest budgets through creativity, commitment and volunteer networks that hold them up year after year.

That also makes them fragile. Two key people leaving can stop an entire programme.

## How to find them

Searching online is not enough, precisely because the ones that do not invest in visibility are the ones that do not appear.

The usual sources work: public registries to check current status, sector networks, and — most usefully — the neighbourhood community boards and parishes, who know exactly who is working there right now.
