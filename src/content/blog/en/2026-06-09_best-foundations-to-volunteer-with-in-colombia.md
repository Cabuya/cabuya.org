You want to give your time to a serious organization and not discover later that it was not what it claimed, or that your work was not reaching where it should.

Colombia has a mature social ecosystem with thousands of organizations operating with genuine purpose. It also has badly run projects, organizations that lost their way, and a few schemes that exploit good intentions.

Telling them apart does not depend on how well known the foundation is. It depends on three criteria.

## The three marks of a serious organization

**Operational transparency.** It publishes reports, shows how it spends resources, and lets you see its results — including the ones that did not work.

**Formal structure.** Current legal registration, an identifiable administrative team, a defined process for taking volunteers. If nobody knows who will support you, nobody will.

**Continuity.** It has been operating for years. It is not a project that appears and vanishes without explanation.

## The signal that disqualifies immediately

**Being asked for money to volunteer.**

Volunteering is voluntary in both directions: you contribute time, the organization contributes structure. If they demand payment for compulsory training, materials or membership, this is not a serious organization.

Foundations are grateful for your time. They do not charge you for it.

## Six areas short of people

- **Child protection** — homes providing integrated support over years.
- **Rural education** — programmes where state provision is limited or absent.
- **Food and nutrition** — communities facing food insecurity.
- **People living on the street** — night shelters, kitchens, reintegration, basic medical care. Emotionally demanding and among the most transformative work there is.
- **Disability** — requires prior training and sustained commitment. If you trained in therapy, psychology or special education, your contribution is scarce.
- **Environment** — ecosystem restoration, conservation, environmental education. Very different from urban volunteering.

## How to choose

1. **By the cause that moves you**, not by the best-known foundation. Visibility and quality are not correlated.
2. **By proximity**, so it stays sustainable.
3. **By verifying before committing.**

## The two checks that reveal the most

**Visit the premises.** An in-person visit says more than any online profile: how staff treat the people they serve, how the space is organised, how openly they show you the daily operation.

**Talk to current volunteers.** Ask what the day-to-day is actually like, what has been hard, what they wish would improve, and whether they would recommend joining. That answer is worth more than any institutional material.

## Do not settle for the first

Look into several, visit some, compare. It can take weeks, and in exchange it prevents years in the wrong place.

The goal is not to find the best foundation in Colombia. It is to find the place you will want to be at every week.
