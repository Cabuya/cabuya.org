You look for volunteering and find offers designed for working adults, not for someone with classes, exams and semesters.

Opportunities designed for young people do exist. They are just not where people tend to look.

## What you are studying is already a tool

The volunteering students get the most out of is the kind that connects directly to what they are studying.

Studying maths? Support school tutoring in maths. Studying languages? Teach English. Studying law? Sit in on simple cases. You do not have to wait until graduation for what you know to be useful.

## The formats that fit student life

**Digital support.** Running social accounts, creating content, maintaining websites, editing video, handling databases. Many organizations have long-serving teams without those skills and cannot afford to hire them.

It is done from home, with no travel, on a flexible schedule. It is probably the best entry point there is.

**Environmental projects.** Reforestation, clean-ups, environmental education in schools, weekend trips to rural areas. These run as discrete days rather than a weekly commitment, so they fit around an academic calendar.

**University programmes.** Many Colombian universities run official volunteering: coordinated, supported, certified, and sometimes credited academically. Ask student services before searching outside.

**Professional volunteering**, once you have graduated. Legal advice, accounting, communications, engineering. You contribute exactly what the organization cannot pay for.

## Your age is not a disadvantage

Plenty of organizations actively look for young volunteers. Energy, creativity and fluency with technology are scarce in teams that have operated the same way for years.

What you bring naturally is precisely what those organizations are missing.

## What you get, said plainly

The skills you develop here — leadership, working with people very unlike you, resolving conflict, communicating with difficult audiences, running projects — are the ones that later separate a good professional from a mediocre one in any industry.

And they are not learned in a classroom. It is one of the highest-return investments a young person can make.

## The motivation that ruins it

Looking for volunteering **only** for the certificate, the CV line, or an academic requirement.

That instrumental motivation rarely produces anything meaningful, and it shows from day one — to the organization and to the people you are supposedly there to accompany.

The professional benefit arrives anyway. It just arrives as a consequence, not as the objective.
