You see someone asking for help on a corner, you know neighbours going through a bad stretch, and you feel something should be done. But you do not know what kind of help actually works.

Helping well is more complicated than it looks: a poorly considered donation can create dependency, good intentions without structure can overwhelm an organization, and occasional volunteering can build bonds that later break.

## 1. Giving on the street does not always help

It is counterintuitive and worth saying: immediate handouts can feed organised begging circuits that keep people on the street — especially children — rather than getting them off it.

The help that supports a real way out flows through organizations running recovery processes: food, shelter, psychological care, reintegration. None of that fits in a coin.

This does not mean ignoring the person. It means eye contact, a greeting and dignified treatment do matter, and the contribution that changes something travels a different route.

## 2. Prefer structured over spontaneous

An organization can produce changes an individual cannot, however good their intentions: continuity, follow-up, connection to other services, the capacity to respond when a case gets complicated.

Your contribution inside that structure goes several times further than the same handout made on your own.

## 3. Ask what they actually need

A lot of people donate what they **assume** is useful: clothing nobody will wear, expired food, prescription medicines, broken toys.

That creates extra work for the organization, which has to sort, discard and pay to dispose of it. One call beforehand avoids all of it.

## 4. The most valuable thing is rarely material

An hour of tutoring a week does more than new clothes. Legal advice for a migrant family resolves more than a bag of groceries.

Your time and your professional knowledge are scarcer than your money, which is why they are worth more.

## 5. Consistency beats size

Ten people giving a small amount every month sustain far more than one person giving a large amount once.

Organizations can plan when the flow is predictable, even if it is modest. A single large gift is a relief; a small consistent one is an operation.

## What you can do today

- Donate to a verified organization that accepts contributions online.
- Offer time at a weekend session near your home.
- Share information about real causes, to widen their reach.
- Connect someone you know with the organization that can help them.

And there are less visible things that count too: buying from a street vendor instead of ignoring them, treating the people you pass with dignity, talking with your children about the country's social reality, challenging a prejudice when you hear one.

## The biggest mistake

Doing nothing for fear of doing it wrong.

No experienced volunteer or donor started out knowing everything. They learned by doing, getting things wrong, and adjusting. Starting imperfectly and improving beats waiting until you have all the answers.

And if what stops you is the feeling that your contribution is too small: organizations report again and again that the sum of small sustained contributions produces more cumulative impact than large occasional donations.
