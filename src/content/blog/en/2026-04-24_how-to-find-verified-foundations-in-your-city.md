You want to support organizations working **in your area**, not on the other side of the country. That search is quite different from typing "foundations" into a search engine, and so are the tools.

## The official sources

Colombia's business and social registry (**RUES**) lists legally registered non-profits, by city. **Local chambers of commerce** hold information on organizations in their jurisdiction. **Regional and city governments** often publish directories of organizations partnered with their social programmes.

These sources give you formality, not currency. An organization can be listed and have stopped operating three years ago. Use them as a starting point, not as an answer.

## Sector networks

Colombia has associations grouping organizations by theme: family foundations, NGO confederations, regional networks, and sector-specific associations for health, education and the environment.

Their directories have one concrete advantage: members passed through some filter on joining. It is not an audit, but it is more than nothing.

## The source no directory captures

Parishes, community boards and neighbourhood cultural centres know which organizations are working **right now** in their territory.

That hyperlocal information almost never reaches a national database, and it usually points to small and mid-sized foundations doing deep work in specific areas. Those tend to be exactly where support is most needed.

## The networks you already have

- Professionals in your field who already do social work.
- Companies in your city with established corporate responsibility programmes.
- Nearby universities with formal community outreach.

These informal networks know the reliable organizations in the territory better than any directory, because they have worked with them.

Attending sector gatherings when your city hosts one also helps. There you can ask whoever runs an organization directly, which is a form of verification that is hard to replace.

## How to verify what you find

1. A current legal registration in the public databases.
2. Recent mentions in media, or social accounts with genuine interaction.
3. Direct contact: judge how professional the reply is and how long it takes.
4. If possible, visit their facilities or an active project before committing to anything significant.

## Do not pick the first one

Identify three or four verified options aligned with causes you care about. Contact or visit each. Compare.

That exploration takes a few weeks and saves you months of involvement in the wrong place. Choosing in a hurry ends in disillusionment, and disillusionment keeps people away from volunteering for years.
