You want a picture of which organizations are operating in Colombia today and you find old lists carrying the names of entities that closed years ago.

We publish no list here, and the reason is the same one that has you searching: **any roster published today will be out of date within a year.** Organizations close, new ones appear, some change focus. A static list is a promise nobody can keep.

What does help is knowing which sectors to search in and which sources stay alive.

## The nine sectors

- **Children and young people** — from early childhood through the transition to adult life.
- **Health** — institutions combining care with research, plus hundreds of smaller organizations in childhood cancer, rare diseases, mental health and disability.
- **Education** — tutoring, technical training, scholarships, student support.
- **Environment** — watersheds, wildlife conservation, ecological restoration, environmental education.
- **Women and gender** — gender violence, sexual and reproductive health, economic autonomy, political participation.
- **Older people** — one of the sectors with the fastest-growing need and the least media attention relative to the population it serves.
- **Reconciliation and conflict victims** — rebuilding social fabric and memory in affected territories.
- **People living on the street** — shelters, kitchens, reintegration. The most invisible group in the country.
- **Migrant populations** — regularisation, school integration, access to healthcare and work.

Identifying your sector cuts thousands of options down to dozens. That is the filter with the highest return.

## The sources that stay current

**The business and social registry (RUES)** and the chambers of commerce: they tell you who is legally constituted and current. Formal rather than operational information — but it rules things out fast.

**Sector networks.** NGO confederations, foundation associations and regional networks maintain directories of members who passed some filter.

**Neighbourhood community boards and parishes.** They know who is working in that territory right now. It is the most current source there is and the one no database captures.

**Platforms that verify before listing** — provided they publish their verification criteria. If they do not, it is just another list.

## The real size of the ecosystem

Several thousand organizations hold current legal status and verifiable operations in Colombia. The vast majority are unknown to the general public and sustain services for millions of people.

That is good news for you: it means there is far more to choose from than the ten organizations that appear in the media.

## Do not wait for the perfect list

An imperfect list used today produces more than the perfect list awaited next year.

Start with the organizations you already know, add the ones you discover, and let participation itself lead you to the next. That is how almost everyone who has been at this for years got there.
