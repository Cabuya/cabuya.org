You want to get involved in something concrete and nearby, where turning up regularly makes a visible difference. And you run into scattered information, forms nobody answers, and the feeling that helping is complicated when it should be simple.

There are probably active projects less than fifteen minutes from your house that you do not know exist. The problem is not a shortage of projects: it is the disconnection between people who want to help and projects that need support, living a few blocks apart without knowing it.

## 1. Define your real radius

How far are you genuinely willing to travel on a regular basis? If it is your neighbourhood only, search there. If it is your whole district, widen it.

Being honest about that limit keeps you from committing to something you will later abandon over distance, which is the number one cause of dropping out.

## 2. Work out what is nearby

Four categories cover nearly everything:

- **Educational** — tutoring, workshops, community libraries.
- **Food** — kitchens, food distribution, urban gardens.
- **Accompaniment** — older people, children, people with disabilities.
- **Community** — improving public spaces, clean-ups, cultural events.

Each asks for a different level of time and emotional commitment. Choosing the category well matters as much as choosing the organization well.

## 3. Make contact first

Do not wait to be called. Call, visit the premises if there are any, attend an open event, ask what they need.

Organizations receive a great many forms and very few direct approaches. That initiative opens doors far faster than any well-written application.

## 4. Give it three months before judging

The first weeks are always strange: you are learning the dynamics, meeting people, understanding processes.

If you judge the experience after a month, you are not seeing the project. You are seeing your own adaptation curve.

## 5. Contribute what you can do, not just hours

If you teach, offer classes. If you are a doctor, offer clinics. If you work in marketing, run their social accounts. If you do accounting, keep their records.

Specialised volunteering almost always produces more than donating open-ended hours, and it is what the organization cannot obtain any other way.
