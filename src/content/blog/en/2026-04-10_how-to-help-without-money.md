You want to help and your finances do not allow for meaningful donations. From there comes the conclusion that without money you cannot really contribute.

That is false, in a quite specific way: several of the things you can give today are worth more than a small donation, because they buy something money buys badly.

## Your time

It is the most valuable resource you have.

Your consistent presence — week after week, accompanying a child, talking with an older person, supporting an education programme — changes lives in a way a small donation cannot replicate.

And unlike money, it cannot be substituted with more money.

## Your professional knowledge

If you are a lawyer, doctor, accountant, communicator, engineer, designer, teacher, therapist, mechanic or electrician, your knowledge is exactly what many foundations cannot afford.

One consultation from you replaces fees the organization simply does not have. It is the contribution with the best effort-to-impact ratio there is.

## Your contacts

Connecting a foundation to someone who can help has a multiplier effect: a business owner who can contribute, a media outlet that gives visibility, a supplier with better prices, a professional who can advise them.

Any one connection can be worth more than any individual donation of yours.

## Your digital skills

Running social accounts, creating content, designing assets, editing video, maintaining a website, setting up a campaign. These are critical skills most organizations cannot hire for.

Two hours a week here can change the reach of an entire foundation.

## Your voice

Sharing information about serious organizations, making problems visible, connecting people with whoever can help them.

Zero cost. Your personal account becomes a tool for impact if you decide to use it deliberately rather than reactively.

## What you already own and do not use

Clothing, books, toys, furniture, appliances that work.

Before throwing out or selling, ask whether a foundation can give them a second life. It costs you nothing additional.

## The resources of whatever you do

If you run a business, offer special terms to families a foundation supports. If you have a vehicle, occasional transport for materials. If you have an office, your meeting room for organizations with nowhere to meet.

All of that already exists and you already pay for it. Giving it an additional social use costs nothing.

## Your company

Loneliness is one of the great contemporary social problems.

Visiting a care home and listening. Spending a play session with children. Keeping someone in hospital company. Human presence with no agenda: nothing material replaces it.

## Your immediate influence

Talking with your children about social reality. Challenging a prejudice when you hear one. Encouraging someone to get involved.

Every person you bring closer to this indirectly multiplies what you contribute.

---

## A calculation worth doing

If an hour of your professional work has a market price, that same hour donated has the same economic value.

Doing that arithmetic changes the conversation. You are not helping *less* because you gave no money: you are giving something quantifiable that often exceeds what you could have transferred.

Spending an hour a week with an older person who is alone is a service the market does not sell well at any price.
