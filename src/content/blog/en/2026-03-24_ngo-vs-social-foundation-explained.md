You hear "NGO" and "social foundation" used as if they were the same, and you also hear that they are not. In the end you do not know what the organization you want to support actually is, or whether it changes anything.

There are real differences. It is worth knowing them, even though the conclusion of this article is that they are not the ones that should decide your support.

## NGO is a category, not a type

**NGO** stands for non-governmental organization. It is an umbrella term: it covers any legally constituted organization operating independently of government and without profit motive.

Foundations, corporations, associations and social-purpose cooperatives all fit under it. Saying "we are an NGO" tells you nothing about the legal form.

## A foundation is a specific form

A **foundation** is constituted around an **endowment** dedicated to a specific purpose, contributed by one or more founders. That endowment is the base it operates from and must be preserved or grown to sustain the mission.

That is the distinguishing feature: a foundation is organised around assets committed to an end.

## The other forms you will meet

- **Association.** A group of people joined by a shared purpose. It requires no initial endowment and governance sits with the members, who have a vote. Many organizations we call foundations are technically associations.
- **Non-profit corporation.** Similar to an association: based on people rather than assets, with a flexible structure for organizations where participation is central.
- **Cooperative.** Economic in purpose, but it can carry a substantial social component when it serves a vulnerable population or performs a solidarity-economy function.

## Why many call themselves NGOs without technically being one

Because the term is internationally recognised and carries weight in cooperation funding. It is not deception; it is pragmatic use of language.

If you want to know exactly which form it is, ask, or look at the founding document. A serious organization will tell you without hedging.

## Where the difference does matter

In internal logic, not in quality:

- **Foundations** tend to be more stable, because the endowment protects them through a bad year.
- **Associations** tend to be more participatory, because members take part in decisions.
- **Corporations** combine elements of both.

It also changes the relationship with the state. The more independent organizations often act as a counterweight and a critic of public policy; the ones with assets and stable structure often collaborate on larger joint projects. Both functions are needed.

## The five criteria that actually decide

The legal form matters far less than this, and this applies to all of them:

1. That it is legally constituted and current.
2. That its financial information is accessible.
3. That its mission and its actions match.
4. That the team is professional and identifiable.
5. That its impact in its territory is verifiable.

A badly run foundation is a bad option. A well-run association is an excellent one. The legal form does not settle it.
