You want to help. You have felt it for a while. But when you try to take the first step you hit a wall: you do not know where to start, the information is scattered, and you cannot tell which organizations are still active. You search for "volunteering" and get foundations that closed years ago, forms nobody answers, and dead links.

That initial frustration is why a lot of people give up before really starting. It is not a lack of will. It is the lack of a clear path.

## Step 1 — Decide which cause bothers you

Not all volunteering serves the same thing or asks the same of you. Before looking for an organization, ask yourself which problem makes you uncomfortable when you see it. Children? Older people? Education? The environment? Animals?

That answer takes a hundred options down to five, and the five that remain are the ones you will actually keep doing.

## Step 2 — Be honest about the time you have

This is where almost everyone gets it wrong at the start. They promise every weekend and stop showing up within a month.

**Two consistent hours a week are worth more than eight hours one Saturday and never again.** Organizations plan around your time: when someone stops coming, someone else has to cover. Start small and keep it.

## Step 3 — Look where the information is verified

For years volunteering in Colombia was chaotic because there was no single reliable place to find real options. Today there are platforms that centralise opportunities and filter out what is not legitimate.

That saves weeks of searching and, more importantly, keeps your first attempt from landing on an organization that no longer exists.

## Step 4 — Get ready for the first day

Volunteering is not only donating time. It is exposing yourself to realities you have probably never seen up close. A first visit to a shelter or a community kitchen leaves a mark.

Do not go expecting to feel good. Go prepared to come out different.

## Step 5 — Commit to the process, not the event

Most people show up once and leave with a photograph. That contributes very little.

The difference gets built when you come back the following week, learn names, understand specific stories, and your presence becomes part of somebody's routine. That continuity is what changes anything.

## The pattern that keeps repeating

Almost everyone who has done this for years describes the same thing: they started full of doubts, spent months adjusting, and then did not want to stop.

What shows up again and again is that consistent presence at a single organization transforms the volunteer as much as the people they accompany. Consistency builds relationships, relationships build trust, and without trust there is nothing.

## The mistake that kills the most volunteering

Looking for the perfect organization before starting. The ideal schedule, the cause that matches your values a hundred per cent, the right moment. While you wait, the months pass.

Nobody feels ready on day one. Real preparation happens by doing, not by reading. Any volunteer with years behind them will tell you their first day felt awkward and out of place, and that the feeling was gone within weeks.

There will be beautiful days and days when you feel you contributed nothing. Both are part of it. Holding on through the swings is what builds real impact.
