Right now there are projects running on fewer resources than they need. Every week organizations have specific needs that timely support would resolve.

Much of that information never reaches the people willing to help, simply because there is no channel connecting the two in time.

## What kind of support gets asked for

Not all of it is money. Active projects usually need:

- Volunteers for day-to-day operations.
- Donations of specific goods.
- Professionals contributing technical knowledge.
- Visibility to reach more people.

There is almost always a form of support that matches what you have.

## Where the open needs are

**Children.** Tutoring for those who repeated a year, supplementary feeding in schools, life-skills workshops, psychosocial support, safe spaces outside school hours.

**Older people.** Home visits to people in isolation, activities at care homes, preventive health brigades, technology workshops, accompaniment to medical procedures for those with no family network.

**Health.** Preventive clinics where access is difficult, eye and dental brigades, community mental health support, accompanying cancer patients and their families.

**Environment.** Reforestation in critical watersheds, urban river clean-ups, environmental education in schools, community wildlife monitoring, waste management where no formal system exists.

**Employment inclusion.** Technical training for young people without access to higher education, entrepreneurship for women survivors of violence, employment support for people with disabilities, inclusion of migrant populations.

**Reconciliation and conflict victims.** Psychosocial support, historical memory workshops, support for return processes, training for community leaders.

## Three steps to connect

1. **Choose the sector** that resonates most.
2. **Decide what you can contribute**: time, resources, technical knowledge, goods, or reach.
3. **Use a source that stays current**, rather than trawling social media or asking around.

## A model that works well

A group of professionals deciding to contribute as a team twice a month, picking a different project each time.

In six months they encounter fifteen different realities and end up identifying two or three organizations they want to stay with long term — with judgement built on experience rather than assumption.

It is a way of exploring without committing before you know.

## The mistake that costs the most time

Waiting to know exactly which project to support before starting.

That analysis paralysis can last years. Real preferences emerge through practice, not reasoning: starting today with something imperfect is the best strategy for finding out where you want to be.
