You have decided to donate, you know who to, and you know the risks. What is missing is the operational sequence: how to do it so it stays documented and the money arrives whole.

It looks bureaucratic. It is the difference between a donation that disappears and one that produces what you intended.

## 1. Look into them before transferring

Do not donate on impulse. Give it at least a week: website, social accounts, media mentions, other donors' accounts of working with them.

Legitimate foundations withstand any scrutiny without discomfort. The ones with appearance and little substance do not.

## 2. Contact the organization first

A call or an email confirms it is active, connects you to real people, and lets you ask specific questions.

Serious organizations answer professionally. Doubtful ones evade or push you to donate quickly without further conversation. The difference shows in the first exchange.

## 3. Ask for the complete information

The **institutional** bank account, the tax id, and the details needed to issue the donation certificate.

If you get vague answers or excuses for not providing them, that is the end of it. Move to another option.

## 4. Transfer through your channels, not theirs

Use your bank's official app. Never a link that arrives by message.

Check the account number and the beneficiary name twice, and keep the receipt. That record is your accounting and legal protection.

## 5. Ask for the donation certificate

In Colombia, donations to legally constituted foundations can carry a tax effect, and the certificate is the document that supports it with the tax authority.

If the organization delays or makes excuses about issuing it, something is out of order. A certificate takes days, not months.

## 6. Follow up

Serious organizations keep their donors informed: how the resources were used, what results followed, what is next.

If you donate and never hear anything again, that has already told you something about how they operate.

## For large donations: put it in writing

Specify which project it funds, what results are expected, and what reporting you will receive.

It is standard practice among corporate donors and it protects both sides. It also raises the odds of the money ending up where you intended.

## Three habits worth more than the amount

**A fixed monthly budget.** Consistency produces more cumulative impact than one large occasional gift, and it lets the organization plan around what it can count on.

**Split between two or three organizations.** It reduces risk and widens reach. An annual budget split three ways is three meaningful donations, not three crumbs.

**Review every quarter.** It is how you notice a foundation changing direction, or a cause that needs your support more now. Giving well is an active practice, not a standing order.
